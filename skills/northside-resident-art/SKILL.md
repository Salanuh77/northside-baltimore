# Northside resident art

Read the current East Brickton game-page gallery when revisiting the reference. Look at a group shot, a face close-up, and clothing from behind. The Season 4 gallery shows rounded Roblox heads, simple dark facial marks, hair with a continuous silhouette, sleeves ending above visible hands, and layered street clothes with distinct waistbands. At street distance, a beard, jacket collar or hood identifies the resident before small texture details resolve. [Official game and gallery](https://www.roblox.com/games/1923555882/East-Brickton)

Translate these observations into original Baltimore outfits. On the Northside starter body, keep a broad shirt front, a readable head, separate shoes and a visible hem over the waistband. Use Northside’s own fabric, lettering and denim. Give hair enough mass across the crown; check the forehead and nape in profile. Let the base face carry the expression and fit selected detail or facial hair to that head.

Use the same articulated template and dressing routine for the file preview, live resident, barber and respawn. Check starter options and migrated files with older outfit IDs. Look at a front, back and three-quarter view of a dressed resident under daylight and street lighting. A passing mesh-load check establishes availability; inspect a rendered view to judge the silhouette, hands, hair and garments.

Try the smallest GT and the Foundry V8 with a tall resident and a high-volume haircut. Measure the top of the visible hair relative to the cabin roof, then drive and exit. Check a phone viewport with the file picker and face close-up as well as the desktop layout.

Use an original GTA Online-inspired editor: a compact charcoal sidebar, clear white selection rows, restrained mint accents and a large unobstructed character preview. Keep name entry and appearance as separate steps. Group appearance under Face, Hair, Body and Outfit; focus the camera on the head for face/hair and show the full body for outfit/body. Preserve free-text fictional names, curated suggestions, save-file selection, rotation and barber reuse. On narrow portrait screens, stack the preview above the editor; on landscape mobile, keep both usable without overlapping. Check every tab and the actual controls at the smallest supported viewport.

Facial artwork needs to follow the head surface in three-quarter view. Flat cards can look acceptable head-on while floating beyond the cheeks in profile. Use the per-sculpt conforming eye and lip meshes, keep those parts noncolliding and shadow-free, and verify every spacing/fullness choice. Compare source-space front/profile renders and in-engine asset loading separately; a source render is not a gameplay screenshot. Preserve the same face and groom routine for creator, barber and live characters.

Puffs need a gathered base and a distinct lifted crown; low puffs belong at the nape. Two-strand twists need individual lifted ropes and hanging tips rather than tracks pressed against the scalp. Check every selectable haircut, including shaved and the imported Meshy fringe, and retain natural dye options.

## Characters in the gallery

Baltimore Streets 2’s public gallery frames Roblox residents close to the camera. A strong red or blue side light separates groups, and one gesture carries the scene at thumbnail size. For Baltimore Hood RP use the harbor varsity resident on the lead cover. Other compositions can follow the cashier and customer across a register, a resident and officer on a stoop, or a visitor reaching for a bail receipt. Keep the gestures legible against the background and use the game’s own clothing, storefronts and streets. Keep illustrations identified as such in gallery alt text and retain actual gameplay views alongside them. [Reference experience](https://www.roblox.com/games/104551952779032)

When fitting a Meshy groom or uniform accessory, inspect the silhouette from front and profile, trim bust geometry from the hair asset, and attach only the finished cap or hair to the head. Share that fit between the creator, barber and staff where the role uses the same piece.
